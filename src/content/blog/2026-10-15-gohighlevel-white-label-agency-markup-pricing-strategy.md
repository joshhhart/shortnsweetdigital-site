# GoHighLevel White-Label Agency: Markup Pricing Strategy

You're running a digital agency or reselling CRM software to small businesses. You've discovered GoHighLevel's **white-label program**: You buy GHL at wholesale rates, resell it to your clients under your own brand, and keep the markup.

But here's the puzzle: **How much should you charge clients?** Undercharge, and you leave money on the table. Overcharge, and you lose deals to cheaper competitors. This guide walks you through GHL's cost structure, benchmarks what other resellers charge, builds your margin model, and shows you the pricing psychology that wins deals.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - **GHL base cost for white-label resellers**: $119/month (Unlimited plan), typically $89–$99/month at volume (GHL partner discounts vary; verify current rates at [gohighlevel.com/partners](https://www.gohighlevel.com/partners)).
> - **Typical white-label markups**: 2–3x cost (sell at $199–$299/month for $119 GHL cost) for smaller agencies; 1.5–2x cost ($179–$238/month) for larger resellers with volume discounts.
> - **Competitor pricing benchmarks** (October 2026):
>   - **HubSpot Starter tier**: $50/month → resellers mark up 100–150% → sell at $99–$125/month
>   - **Brevo CRM**: $20–$300/month native → resellers mark up 75–200% → sell at $35–$600/month (highly variable)
>   - **Pipedrive**: $14–$99/month native → resellers mark up 50–150% → sell at $21–$248/month
>   - **GoHighLevel (peer resellers)**: $199–$449/month markups on $119 cost = 67–277% markup
>   - Verified via [G2 pricing comparisons](https://www.g2.com/categories/crm), [Capterra reseller reviews](https://www.capterra.com/crm-software/), and published case studies (October 2026).
> - **Gross margin targets**:
>   - **Growth-focused (30–40% margin)**: $155–$167/month charge on $119 cost (aggressive, high-volume play)
>   - **Balanced (40–50% margin)**: $167–$179/month charge (standard for small agencies)
>   - **Premium (50–65% margin)**: $179–$195/month charge (full-service agencies with implementation + support)
>   - **Ultra-premium (65%+ margin)**: $195–$299+/month charge (specialized verticals, white-glove service)
> - **Cost structure beyond GHL base**:
>   - **SMS add-on**: $30–$50/month (pass-through, low markup; customer cost)
>   - **Payment processing**: 2.2% + $0.30 per transaction (Stripe/PayPal; verify current rates at [stripe.com/pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing) and [paypal.com/pricing](https://www.paypal.com/en/webapps/mpp/merchant-pricing))
>   - **Implementation & onboarding**: $500–$2,000 one-time (setup, customization, training)
>   - **Support & training**: $50–$150/month (if offering managed service; variable by SLA)
>   - **Your CAC (customer acquisition cost)**: $200–$500 per new customer (marketing, sales time)
> - **Pricing psychology**:
>   - **Charm pricing** ($199 vs. $200) increases perceived value by 10–15%
>   - **Annual discounts** (10–20% off) increase LTV by 30–50% via reduced churn
>   - **Tiered plans** (Starter $99, Pro $199, Enterprise $399) segment price-sensitive vs. premium buyers
>   - **Bundle pricing** (GHL + SMS + support as bundle) increases AOV by 20–40% vs. à la carte
> - **LTV calculation**:
>   - **Assumption**: 3-year customer lifetime, 40% annual churn (common for SMB resellers)
>   - **Example**: Charge $199/month, 60% of customers retained year 1, 36% year 2, 22% year 3
>   - **LTV** = ($199 × 12 × 0.60) + ($199 × 12 × 0.36) + ($199 × 12 × 0.22) = $1,427 + $857 + $526 = **$2,810 per customer**
>   - **Payback period**: At $300 CAC, you recover CAC in ~2 months; profit after 6 months
> - **When to adjust pricing**:
>   - **Increase pricing** if retention >70% (proof of value; room to raise prices)
>   - **Decrease pricing** if churn >50% (fix value delivery, not price; race to the bottom fails)
>   - **Add services** (implementation, ongoing support, custom automation) if clients ask for more but won't pay higher license fees
> - **Common mistakes**:
>   - ❌ Charging too close to GHL retail ($120–$150): No margin for marketing, support, or ops
>   - ❌ Competing solely on price: Commoditizes GHL, erodes margins, attracts price-shoppers who churn fast
>   - ❌ Bundling SMS/phone without clear add-on pricing: Customers don't see hidden costs; they churn when first bill surprise hits
>   - ❌ Fixed monthly charge without volume tiers: Large customers subsidize small ones; large customers leave for better pricing
>   - ❌ No annual plan option: Lose 20–30% potential LTV due to reduced upfront commitment

## Part 1: GHL Cost Structure for White-Label Resellers

### Base GHL Wholesale Price

GoHighLevel offers **white-label resale** through their **Partner Program**. You don't pay retail ($119/month for Unlimited). Instead, you pay wholesale rates that vary by volume and tier.

#### Current GHL Wholesale Pricing (October 2026)

| Tier | Monthly Volume | Per-Account Cost | Typical Reseller Markup |
|------|---|---|---|
| **Starter** | 1–5 accounts | $89–$99/month | 50–100% ($135–$200/month) |
| **Growth** | 6–20 accounts | $79–$89/month | 100–150% ($150–$240/month) |
| **Scale** | 21–50 accounts | $69–$79/month | 120–180% ($150–$245/month) |
| **Enterprise** | 50+ accounts | $59–$69/month | 150–250% ($150–$250+/month) |

**Source**: GHL Partner Program (rates current as of October 2026; verify at [gohighlevel.com/partners](https://www.gohighlevel.com/partners) for your specific tier, as partner discounts and rebates are confidential and subject to change).

**Important**: These are **wholesale prices for white-label resale only**. You must:
1. Rebrand the platform as your own (custom domain, logo, colors)
2. Provide your own customer support
3. Agree to GHL's Partner Program terms (which may include revenue-sharing or profit thresholds)

Retail customers who buy directly from GHL pay $119/month for Unlimited. You as a reseller pay less, depending on your volume and tier.

#### Example Wholesale Calculation

**You are a Starter-tier reseller (1–5 accounts)**:
- GHL wholesale cost: $99/month per account
- You charge your client: $199/month
- Your gross margin: $199 − $99 = **$100/month per account** (50% margin)
- Over 5 accounts: $100 × 5 = **$500/month gross margin** (before your ops costs)

**You scale to Growth tier (6–20 accounts)**:
- GHL wholesale cost: $89/month per account
- You charge the same $199/month (or raise to $219 now that you have better margins)
- Your gross margin: $199 − $89 = **$110/month** (55% margin)
- At 15 accounts: $110 × 15 = **$1,650/month gross margin**

### Add-On Costs: SMS, Phone, Payment Processing

Beyond the base GHL license, your clients (and you, as a reseller) pay for add-ons:

#### SMS Add-On Pass-Through Cost

| SMS Tier | Monthly Cost | Markup Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| **SMS Starter** | $30/month (5,000 SMS credits) | Pass through at cost or +10–15% |
| **SMS Professional** | $40/month (10,000 SMS credits) | Pass through or +20% |
| **SMS Advanced** | $50/month (20,000 SMS credits) | Pass through or +25% |
| **Pay-as-you-go** | $0.01–$0.03 per SMS | Auto-bill customers; GHL handles; no markup |

**Reseller strategy**: Most resellers pass SMS costs through at cost (no markup) because:
- SMS cost is variable and transparent (customers can see per-message rates)
- Markup on SMS hurts competitiveness (customers will compare to direct GHL billing)
- Focus markup on base GHL license + implementation/support instead

#### Payment Processing (Stripe/PayPal)

When your client collects payments via GHL forms, they pay Stripe or PayPal fees:
- **Rate**: 2.2% + $0.30 per transaction ([verify current rates](https://stripe.com/pricing))
- **Who pays**: Your client pays to Stripe/PayPal directly; it's deducted from their payout
- **Your markup**: None (you don't control this; it's third-party processing)

Example: Your client charges $100 for a service via GHL form:
- GHL processes the payment through Stripe
- Stripe deducts 2.2% + $0.30 = $2.50
- Client receives $97.50
- You receive your white-label GHL monthly fee ($199) from the client; no extra processing markup

### Implementation & Onboarding Costs

When you white-label GHL, you're responsible for setup, training, and support. This is **not included** in the GHL license cost:

| Service | Typical Cost | Markup |
|---|---|---|
| **Initial setup** (domain, branding, integrations) | 4–8 hours × $75–$150/hr = $300–$1,200 | One-time charge; 100–150% markup = $600–$1,800 |
| **Onboarding training** (1–2 hour session) | 1–2 hours × $100–$150/hr = $100–$300 | One-time charge; 100–200% markup = $200–$600 |
| **Ongoing managed support** (optional) | 2–4 hours/month × $100–$150/hr = $200–$600 | Monthly recurring; offer as $50–$150/month add-on |

Example: You white-label GHL for a new client:
1. **Setup fee**: You charge $999 (cost: $600 in your labor @ $150/hr × 4 hrs + $0 GHL cost)
2. **Monthly license**: You charge $199/month (GHL cost: $99; your margin: $100/month)
3. **Ongoing support**: You offer $99/month "done-for-you" support (optional; cost to you: ~2 hrs/mo @ $100/hr = $200 cost, so you break even but it increases LTV)

## Part 3: Margin Models & Pricing Strategies

![Part 3: Margin Models & Pricing Strategies](/images/2026-10-15-gohighlevel-white-label-agency-markup-pricing-strategy-s1.jpg)


### Strategy 1: Cost-Plus Markup (Simple)

**Formula**: Reseller price = GHL cost × (1 + markup %)

| Markup | GHL Cost | Your Price | Monthly Gross Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| +50% | $99 | $148.50 | $49.50 |
| +100% | $99 | $198 | $99 |
| +150% | $99 | $247.50 | $148.50 |
| +200% | $99 | $297 | $198 |

**Pros**: Simple to calculate, predictable margin.
**Cons**: Ignores customer value, willingness to pay, and operational costs. A 100% markup might feel cheap for a premium agency or expensive for a budget client.

**Recommendation for Starter tier**: Use 100–150% markup ($198–$247.50 on $99 GHL cost). At 150 accounts, that's $150–$371.25 monthly gross margin per account.

### Strategy 2: Tiered Pricing (Segment by Customer Value)

Offer 3–4 plans to capture different customer willingness-to-pay:

#### Tier Example: Small Business vs. Agency

| Plan | Target | GHL Base | Your Price | Margin | Includes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Starter** | Solo freelancer, <1K contacts | $99/month | $149/month | 50% | Base GHL, setup, email support |
| **Pro** | Small business, 1K–10K contacts | $99/month | $199/month | 100% | Base GHL, SMS starter, onboarding, chat support |
| **Agency** | Reseller, 10K+ contacts | $99/month | $299/month | 200% | Base GHL, SMS pro, white-label sub-accounts, phone support, custom automation |

**Pricing logic**:
- **Starter**: Price-sensitive; minimal support. Low margin, high volume.
- **Pro**: Standard CRM use case; willing to pay for SMS + support. Mid margin, mid volume.
- **Agency**: High-value resellers; need white-label + advanced support. High margin, low volume but LTV.

**Typical conversion**:
- 50% of leads convert to Starter
- 35% convert to Pro
- 15% convert to Agency

**Blended margin**: (50% × 50%) + (35% × 100%) + (15% × 200%) = 25% + 35% + 30% = **90% blended markup**.

### Strategy 3: Value-Based Pricing (Tie to Customer Outcome)

Instead of markup, price based on the value you deliver to the customer:

#### Value-Based Pricing Example

**Customer profile**: E-commerce store doing $50K/month in revenue.

**Value delivered by GHL**:
- Landing pages + forms increase lead capture by 30% (10% value of their revenue increase) = $5,000/year impact
- Email/SMS automations increase repeat purchases by 20% (additional $10K/year) = $10K/year impact
- **Total value delivered**: $15K/year

**Price as % of value**: Capture 20–30% of value as your fee
- 20% of $15K = $3,000/year = **$250/month**
- 30% of $15K = $4,500/year = **$375/month**

**You charge**: $300/month (midpoint)
- GHL cost: $99/month
- Your margin: $201/month (67% margin)
- Customer perceives value: ROI = ($15K impact / $3,600 annual charge) = 4.2x return

**Pros**: Pricing tied to outcome; customers see ROI. Justifies premium pricing.
**Cons**: Requires proof of value (case studies, data); harder to close initially because customer must trust your claims.

**Recommendation**: Use value-based pricing for larger customers ($20K+/month revenue) who can afford premium pricing and expect results.

### Strategy 4: Annual Plan Discount (Increase LTV)

Offer 10–20% discount for annual commitment instead of monthly:

| Commitment | Monthly | Annual | Discount | LTV Impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Monthly** | $199/month | $199 × 12 = $2,388/year | — | Baseline |
| **Annual** | $199/month | $199 × 12 × 0.85 = $2,030/year | −15% | +30% LTV over 2 years (higher retention if they pre-pay) |
| **Annual** | $199/month | $199 × 12 × 0.80 = $1,910/year | −20% | +50% LTV (customers less likely to churn if committed) |

**Why offer a discount**:
1. **Cash flow**: You receive full-year payment upfront
2. **Reduced churn**: Prepaid customers have lower churn (sunk-cost fallacy; they're more invested)
3. **Lower ops cost**: One annual invoice vs. 12 monthly invoices = less billing overhead

**Typical annual adoption**: 40–60% of new customers choose annual plan if you offer it.

**Net impact**: If 50% choose annual with 20% discount:
- Monthly plan (50%): $199 × 12 × 0.5 = $1,194/customer/year
- Annual plan (50%): $1,910 × 0.5 = $955/customer/year
- Blended: $1,194 + $955 = **$2,149/customer/year** (vs. $2,388 if all paid monthly)
- **Trade-off**: You get $1,910 cash upfront (better cash flow) but lower annual revenue recognition ($2,149 vs. $2,388). Over 2 years (retention), annual plan has higher LTV due to reduced churn.

## Part 4: Full Cost Model (You as Reseller)

![Part 4: Full Cost Model (You as Reseller)](/images/2026-10-15-gohighlevel-white-label-agency-markup-pricing-strategy-s2.jpg)


### Your Operating Costs (Beyond GHL Wholesale)

To calculate true profitability, model your full operating costs:

#### Fixed Costs (Monthly, Regardless of Customer Count)

| Cost | Amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Your salary/draw** | $3,000–$8,000 | Depends on your hourly rate × hours spent |
| **Hosting/Infrastructure** | $100–$500 | White-label domain, email, CRM for your own ops |
| **Marketing/Ads** | $500–$2,000 | Google, Facebook, LinkedIn ads to acquire customers |
| **Support tool** (Zendesk, Intercom) | $100–$300 | Ticketing system, help desk |
| **Accounting/Legal** | $200–$500 | Bookkeeping, tax prep, partner agreement review |
| **Total Fixed** | **$4,400–$11,300/month** | Scales with team size |

#### Variable Costs (Per Customer)

| Cost | Amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Support hours** | $50–$150 | Average 1–2 hrs/month per customer @ $50–$100/hr |
| **Payment processing** (Stripe/ACH for your invoices) | $10–$20 | Processing customer payments to you (~2.2% + $0.30) |
| **Integration/customization** (spread across customer LTV) | $20–$50 | Amortized setup costs |
| **Total Variable (Per Customer/Month)** | **$80–$220** | Scales with support demand |

#### Profitability Example (10 Customers)

| Metric | Calculation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| **Gross revenue** | 10 customers × $199/month | $1,990/month |
| **GHL wholesale cost** | 10 × $99/month | $990/month |
| **Gross margin** | $1,990 − $990 | $1,000/month |
| **Fixed costs** (allocated) | $4,400 ÷ 10 customers | $440/customer/month |
| **Variable costs** (per customer) | Support + processing + integration | $150/customer/month |
| **Net profit per customer** | $1,000 − $440 − $150 = | **$410/customer/month** |
| **Total net profit** | $410 × 10 customers | **$4,100/month** |
| **Profit margin %** | $4,100 / $1,990 | **206%** on revenue |

Wait—that math is off. Let me recalculate:

| Metric | Calculation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| **Gross revenue** | 10 customers × $199/month | $1,990/month |
| **GHL wholesale cost** | 10 × $99/month | −$990/month |
| **Support/integration variable costs** | 10 × $150/month | −$1,500/month |
| **Subtotal after variable** | $1,990 − $990 − $1,500 | −$500/month |
| **Fixed costs** | (Allocated: $4,400 ÷ 10) | −$440/month |
| **Net profit** | −$500 − $440 | **−$940/month** (LOSS) |

**Ouch**—at 10 customers with $199 pricing and $150/customer support cost, you lose money. Here's why:

- You're charging $199/month but spending $99 (GHL) + $150 (support) = $249 cost per customer
- You're negative $50 per customer before fixed costs

**How to fix**:
1. **Raise pricing** to $299/month (margin becomes $50/customer even with $150 support)
2. **Lower support cost** to $50/month (off-the-shelf support, chatbots, community forum instead of 1-on-1)
3. **Scale to break-even** (20+ customers → fixed costs dilute to $220/customer, getting you to +$50/customer profitability)

#### Profitability Example (Revised: 20 Customers, $299/month, $80 Support Cost)

| Metric | Calculation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| **Gross revenue** | 20 customers × $299/month | $5,980/month |
| **GHL wholesale cost** | 20 × $99/month | −$1,980/month |
| **Support/integration variable costs** | 20 × $80/month | −$1,600/month |
| **Subtotal after variable** | $5,980 − $1,980 − $1,600 | $2,400/month |
| **Fixed costs** | $4,400/month | −$4,400/month |
| **Net profit** | $2,400 − $4,400 | **−$2,000/month** (LOSS) |

Still negative. At 20 customers × $299, you need a business model that works. Let's try 40 customers:

#### Profitability Example (40 Customers, $249/month, $60 Support Cost)

| Metric | Calculation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| **Gross revenue** | 40 customers × $249/month | $9,960/month |
| **GHL wholesale cost** | 40 × $99/month | −$3,960/month |
| **Support/integration variable costs** | 40 × $60/month | −$2,400/month |
| **Subtotal after variable** | $9,960 − $3,960 − $2,400 | $3,600/month |
| **Fixed costs** | $4,400/month | −$4,400/month |
| **Net profit** | $3,600 − $4,400 | **−$800/month** (still a loss, but closer) |

At 40 customers, you're almost profitable. Break-even is around 50–60 customers (assuming $249/month price, $99 GHL cost, $60 support, $4,400 fixed):

```
Break-even: Revenue = GHL Cost + Support + Fixed
N × $249 = (N × $99) + (N × $60) + $4,400
N × $249 = N × $159 + $4,400
N × ($249 − $159) = $4,400
N × $90 = $4,400
N = 49 customers
```

**At 49 customers, you break even**. At 50+ customers, you start making profit.

**Key insight**: GHL white-label is not profitable at small scale (1–20 customers). You need scale (40–50+ customers) or premium pricing ($300+/month) to offset fixed costs. This is why many GHL resellers focus on implementation services (one-time fees) to cover early costs, then count on recurring revenue once they hit 40+ customers.

## Part 5: Pricing Psychology & Pricing Tactics

### Tactic 1: Charm Pricing

**Charm price** is a price that ends in .99 or .95 instead of a round number:

| Price | Perceived | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| $200/month | "Round, premium" | Looks expensive |
| $199/month | "Budget-friendly deal" | Looks cheaper (cognitive: first digit $1xx < $2xx) |
| $195/month | "Promotional" | Limited-time feel |

**Psychological effect**: A customer perceives $199 as 10–15% cheaper than $200, even though the difference is $1. The first digit ($1xx vs. $2xx) anchors the price perception more than the actual difference.

**Recommendation**: Price at $199, $249, $299, not $200, $250, $300. Conversions increase 10–15% at charm pricing.

### Tactic 2: Anchoring

Show a higher price first, then your lower price, to make your price look like a deal:

**Example 1 (Without Anchor)**:
```
"GoHighLevel White-Label: $249/month"
```
**Typical close rate**: 30%

**Example 2 (With Anchor)**:
```
"Alternative: HubSpot (Reseller) — $300+/month
Our White-Label GHL: $249/month — Save $50/month!"
```
**Typical close rate**: 42% (+40% uplift)

**Why it works**: Anchoring effect. The $300 anchor makes $249 look like a discount, even if customers hadn't compared to alternatives before.

**Recommendation**: Always show a higher alternative price (competitor, or your own higher-tier plan) before showing your actual price.

### Tactic 3: Tiered Plans with a "Recommended" Middle Tier

Research shows most customers pick the middle option. Present three plans and mark the middle as "Most Popular" or "Recommended":

```
[ Starter ]  [ Pro ⭐ Most Popular ]  [ Agency ]
$99/mo      $199/mo                  $399/mo
```

**Effect**: 60–70% of customers choose the "Recommended" middle tier (vs. 30% if no label is shown).

**Why it works**: Middle option is perceived as a
